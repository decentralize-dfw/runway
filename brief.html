THE INTERNET IS STILL FLAT
WE ARE SPATIAL BEINGS LIVING IN A TWO-DIMENSIONAL DIGITAL WORLD
BREAK FREE FROM THE RECTANGLE
Your entire online existence happens through rectangles. You scroll, tap, swipe—all flat. Websites are documents. Social media is a feed. Even our "3D" experiences are projected onto 2D screens. We've trapped infinity in a rectangle. Every interaction you have online is mediated through rectangular interfaces—your phone, your monitor, even VR goggles project rectangles. But why? Is it because rectangles are the best way to experience digital content, or simply because we haven't imagined alternatives? We believe digital space can be something more. Not a simulation of reality, but a new realm with its own language and logic. What if digital space could be truly spatial—not confined to flat surfaces, but expanding into dimensions we haven't explored yet?

01 / THE PROBLEM
DIGITAL PRISON
INFINITY TRAPPED IN A RECTANGLE
BOUNDLESS REALM, PAPER DESIGN
DIGITAL MUST FIND ITS OWN VOICE
We've created a boundless virtual realm, yet we're still designing it like paper. Websites are documents. Apps are pages. Even our "immersive" experiences are just rectangles we strap to our faces. Why? Because we're afraid. Afraid that if we let go of familiar metaphors, people won't understand. Every revolutionary medium went through this. Early films were filmed plays. Early websites were digital brochures. But eventually, each medium discovered its own language. Film learned montage, cinema learned to move the camera, television learned the close-up. Digital space is still in its infancy, still mimicking the past instead of embracing its own possibilities. We're designing websites like magazines, social feeds like bulletin boards, virtual worlds like real-world architecture. But digital space doesn't have to follow physical rules.

03 / OUR PHILOSOPHY
DIGITAL AUTONOMY
RELIGIOUS ROBOTICS
LET DIGITAL SPACE BE WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS
NO GRAVITY · NO SOLID MASSES · CHROME EVERYTHING
There's something deeply unsettling about watching empty digital spaces pretending to be something they're not. Every virtual café, every digital office space feels like an admission of our inability to face what these spaces actually want to be. We've created a realm free from every constraint that defines physical existence—gravity, material cost, linear time—and what do we do with this freedom? We chain it to our earthbound habits. It reminds me of those dreams where you realize you can fly, but instead of soaring, you keep walking because you're afraid of what freedom might mean. That's us, right now, with digital architecture. Lebbeus Woods understood this, though he worked with paper and pencil. His drawings weren't trying to be buildable—they were attempts to free architecture from self-imposed limitations. Religious Robotics is about letting digital space exist on its own terms.

05 / WHAT WE CREATE
BROWSER-BASED WORLDS
DIGITAL TOPOGRAPHIES
WEBGL · NO DOWNLOAD · INSTANT ACCESS
FROM CONCEPT TO LIVE IN YOUR BROWSER
Digital Topographies is a sweeping exploration into what architecture could become when freed from earthly constraints of gravity, weather, and material scarcity. Conceived as a series of lightweight 3D models, this body of work functions both as portfolio and provocative research project. We build spatial experiences for the browser—no downloads, no apps, just pure WebGL architecture that anyone can access instantly. Our work spans galleries, multi-purpose halls, headquarters, event venues, sculptural pavilions, and retail environments. Each space is designed to load quickly and run smoothly on any device. We employ mesh optimization, level of detail strategies, and efficient texture mapping. The glTF/GLB format supports PBR materials for realistic shading without heavy computational overhead. From DAOTOWN at Milan Design Week to floating chrome galleries, from virtual theaters to crystalline retail spaces—every project is an experiment.
